# Epistemic Architecture: Broadcast Mind

## The epistemic categories

The system must explicitly distinguish these categories at
all times. No LLM output may be conflated with truth.

```
FACT
CLAIM
EVIDENCE
ANALYSIS
INTERPRETATION
SPECULATION
FICTION
SATIRE
SYNTHETIC
UNKNOWN
```

## Definitions

### FACT

A statement verified by multiple independent, reliable sources
with high confidence. Facts are append-only in the world state.
Facts may be corrected but never deleted — corrections create
new records that supersede old ones.

**Provenance requirement:** Minimum 2 independent reliable
sources, or 1 highly authoritative source with corroborating
context.

**Confidence threshold:** ≥0.85 (configurable).

### CLAIM

A statement asserted in source material that has not been
verified as fact. Claims may be true or false. The system
tracks claims separately from facts and evaluates them
independently.

**Provenance requirement:** Source URL, extraction method,
extractor identity.

**Confidence threshold:** Not applicable — claims are
evaluated, not assigned confidence until verified.

### EVIDENCE

A piece of source material or analysis that supports or
contradicts a Claim. Evidence is never truth — it is the raw
material for evaluation.

**Provenance requirement:** Source URL, extraction timestamp,
extraction method.

**Mutable:** No — evidence records are immutable.

### ANALYSIS

Structured evaluation of evidence by an Analyst capability.
Analysis is the system's best judgment about what the evidence
means. Analysis is not fact — it is interpretation of evidence.

**Provenance requirement:** Analyst type, input evidence,
timestamp, model used.

**Mutable:** No — analysis is immutable once recorded.

### INTERPRETATION

A persona's reading of a Claim or Event. Interpretations are
opinion by definition — they belong to personas and never
contaminate factual world state.

**Provenance requirement:** Persona ID, source Claim/Event,
timestamp.

**Mutable:** Yes — interpretations may be revised by new
evidence.

### SPECULATION

A hypothesis not yet supported by sufficient evidence.
Speculation is explicitly labeled and tracked separately from
analysis. The system should not broadcast speculation as fact.

**Provenance requirement:** Hypothesis source, evidence gap
description, timestamp.

**Mutable:** Yes — may be promoted to analysis or demoted to
unknown as evidence changes.

### FICTION

Content identified as fabricated or invented. The system
should flag fiction and never treat it as factual. Fiction
may be useful for synthetic segments but must be labeled as
such.

**Provenance requirement:** Detection method, source URL,
timestamp.

### SATIRE

Content identified as satirical or parody. Satire is not fact
and must not be broadcast as news. The system should recognize
satirical sources and label satirical content appropriately.

**Provenance requirement:** Source identification, detection
method, timestamp.

### SYNTHETIC

Content generated by the system itself — scripts, summaries,
opinions, thought experiments. Synthetic content must always
be labeled as synthetic and never presented as factual reporting.

**Provenance requirement:** Generation model, prompt, timestamp,
persona (if applicable).

### UNKNOWN

A state where the system lacks sufficient information to
classify. Unknown is not the same as false — it is a honest
admission of uncertainty. The system should communicate unknown
states clearly in broadcasts.

**Provenance requirement:** Gap description, timestamp.

## Information flow between categories

```
EVIDENCE → ANALYSIS → FACT (if verified)
  ↓            ↓
CLAIM      INTERPRETATION (persona-specific)
  ↓            ↓
SPECULATION  OPINION (persona-held)
  ↓
UNKNOWN (if evidence insufficient)
```

- Evidence supports or contradicts Claims.
- Analysis evaluates Evidence and may upgrade Claims to Facts.
- Interpretation is persona-specific reading of a Claim or Event.
- Opinion is a persona's judgment, always labeled as persona-held.
- Speculation is hypothesis pending evidence.
- Fiction and Satire are identified and labeled at ingestion.
- Synthetic content is labeled at generation time.
- Unknown is the default when evidence is insufficient.

## Provenance requirements

Every record in the world state must carry:

- **source_url** — Where the information came from
- **extracted_at** — When it was extracted
- **extractor** — Which analyst/capability extracted it
- **confidence** — System's confidence in the extraction
- **epistemic_category** — FACT, CLAIM, EVIDENCE, etc.
- **correction_of** — If this record corrects a previous one

## Confidence

Confidence is a float 0.0–1.0 assigned to:

- Evidence reliability (source reliability × recency ×
  corroboration)
- Claim verification status (unverified, partially verified,
  verified)
- Analysis quality (model confidence, analyst type)

Confidence thresholds:

- ≥0.85: Fact (provisionally)
- 0.5–0.84: Claim (requires more evidence)
- <0.5: Unknown or Speculation

Thresholds are configurable but must never be treated as
absolute — confidence is a guide, not a gate.

## Contradiction

Contradiction is detected when:

1. Two Claims about the same entity/event disagree
2. Evidence contradicts a Fact
3. New Evidence invalidates a previous Conclusion

When contradiction is detected:

1. Both positions are retained
2. Confidence scores are adjusted
3. A Correction record is created
4. The broadcast system may note the contradiction
5. Personas may hold different opinions on the contradiction

## Correction

Corrections are append-only records that supersede previous
conclusions without deleting them. A correction must:

1. Reference the record being corrected
2. Provide new evidence or analysis
3. Explain why the previous conclusion was wrong
4. Update confidence scores
5. Be auditable (who/when/why)

## Uncertainty communication

The broadcast system must communicate uncertainty to the user:

- "The system is uncertain about X" — when confidence is low
- "Sources disagree on X" — when contradiction exists
- "This is a claim, not a verified fact" — for unverified claims
- "This analysis is based on limited evidence" — for thin evidence
- "The system does not have enough information to determine X"
  — for unknown states

Uncertainty is never suppressed for the sake of a clean
narrative.

## World state vs. persona interpretation

This is the most critical separation in the architecture:

- **World state** contains Facts, Claims, Evidence, and
  Analysis. It is shared and objective (within epistemic
  limits).
- **Persona interpretation** contains Opinions, Interpretations,
  and Speculation. It is personal and subjective.
- Personas may disagree with each other and with the world
  state. That disagreement is a feature, not a bug.
- Opinions never migrate into world state without evidence.
- World state never tells personas what to think — it only
  provides the evidence.

## Violations to prevent

- Collapsing LLM-generated text into "truth"
- Treating a single source as definitive
- Allowing persona opinions to contaminate factual state
- Suppressing uncertainty to produce a cleaner narrative
- Deleting or mutating old evidence to hide contradictions
- Treating high confidence as certainty
